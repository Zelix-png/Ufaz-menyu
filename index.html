Scientific Calculator

How to use it, step by step
This guide follows the exact key layout of the Deli D82MS scientific calculator — the model most commonly used by UFAZ students (it's essentially identical in function to the Casio fx-82MS family). Below is a labeled diagram of every row of keys, followed by step-by-step instructions and worked examples for every function you'll actually need in class and on the exam.
1
Display — two lines: the top line shows your full typed expression (it scrolls if long), the bottom line shows the result.
2
SHIFT / ALPHA / REPLAY / MODE / CLR / ON — SHIFT (orange) unlocks the orange function printed above a key; ALPHA (red) unlocks memory letters (A–F, X, Y, M); the round REPLAY pad moves the cursor left/right/up/down through your expression; MODE switches calculation modes and angle units; CLR opens a menu to reset settings/memory; ON powers the calculator on.
3
x⁻¹, nCr, pol(, x³ — reciprocal, combinations, rectangular→polar conversion, and cubing. Their SHIFT partners give nPr, rec( and ³√ respectively.
4
a b/c, √, x², ^, log, ln — fractions, square root, squaring, "to the power of", base-10 log, natural log.
5
(-), degree/min/sec, hyp, sin, cos, tan — negative sign, DMS entry for angles, hyperbolic-function toggle, and the three trig keys.
6
RCL, ENG, ( ), M+ — recall a stored memory value, switch to engineering notation, parentheses, and add the shown result into memory (SHIFT+M+ subtracts).
7
7-9, DEL, AC — number keys; DEL removes only the character before the cursor; AC clears the whole calculation.
8
1-3, +, − — number keys plus addition and subtraction.
9
0, ., EXP, Ans, = — decimal point, scientific-notation entry (×10ⁿ), last-answer recall, and the equals key.

1. Turning it on and understanding SHIFT / ALPHA

Press ON to start. Most keys do three things: the white label is the plain function, the orange label above it needs SHIFT first, and the red label needs ALPHA first (used for storing numbers into memory letters like A, B, X, Y).

Tip: After pressing SHIFT or ALPHA, a small "S" or "A" indicator appears at the top of the screen — that's how you know the next key press will use the second/third function.

2. Basic operations & order of operations

Type the expression exactly as you'd write it on paper, then press =. The calculator applies the correct order of operations automatically.

Example: 12 + 7 × 3

12+7×3=
Result: 33 — × is calculated before +, so you don't need to work out 7×3 separately.

3. Parentheses ( )

Use ( and ) to force a different order, exactly like in math.

Example: (12 + 7) × 3

(12+7)×3=
Result: 57

4. Powers & roots (x², x³, ^, √, x⁻¹)

x² squares the shown number, x³ cubes it, ^ raises to any power, √ gives the square root, and x⁻¹ gives the reciprocal (1/x).

Example: 5³

5^3=
Result: 125

Example: √49

√49=
Result: 7

Example: ³√27 (cube root — press SHIFT then x³)

SHIFTx³27=
Result: 3

5. Trigonometric functions (DEG vs RAD)

Before using sin, cos or tan, check your angle unit at the top of the screen. Press MODE repeatedly to pick Deg, Rad, or Gra — most school problems use Deg.

Example: sin(30°) in Deg mode

sin30=
Result: 0.5

For inverse trig (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹), press SHIFT before the trig key. For hyperbolic functions (sinh, cosh, tanh), press hyp first.

Tip: An answer that looks far too big, too small, or negative when it shouldn't be almost always means you're in the wrong angle mode — check Deg/Rad first.

6. Logarithms (log, ln)

log is base-10 logarithm, ln is natural logarithm (base e). SHIFT+log gives 10ˣ, SHIFT+ln gives eˣ.

Example: log(1000)

log1000=
Result: 3

7. Fractions (a b/c)

The a b/c key enters and displays exact fractions instead of decimals, and separates a whole number from its fraction part.

Example: 3/4 + 1/2

3a b/c4+1a b/c2=
Result: 1¼ — press SHIFT+a b/c to toggle between fraction and decimal display.

8. Scientific notation (EXP)

Use EXP to enter "×10^" quickly — essential for very large or small numbers in physics and chemistry.

Example: 3.2 × 10⁵

3.2EXP5=
Result: 320000

9. Combinations & permutations (nCr, nPr)

nCr counts combinations, and SHIFT+nCr gives nPr (permutations) — common in probability and statistics questions.

Example: ⁵C₂ (choose 2 from 5)

5nCr2=
Result: 10

10. Memory & Ans

M+ adds the shown result to memory, SHIFT+M+ subtracts it, RCL then M recalls it, and SHIFT+RCL (labeled CLR from the memory menu) clears memory. Ans re-uses your last calculated answer inside a new expression.

Tip: If the screen shows an "M" symbol, there's a number sitting in memory from a previous problem — clear it before starting a new one so it doesn't sneak into your next calculation.

11. Clearing & fixing mistakes

AC clears everything and starts fresh. DEL deletes only the character just before the cursor, so you can fix a typo without retyping the whole expression. Use the round REPLAY pad to move the cursor left and right into your expression and edit it directly, or up/down to scroll through your last few calculations.
